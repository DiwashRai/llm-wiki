Source: iCanStudy newsletter (Justin), 2026-11-08. "Most people are concerned about AI but still let
it destroy their brain."

## Core idea

- AI gets to answers faster, and is making many smart people worse at their work.
- Struggling through a hard problem yields two things: an answer and a changed brain.
- Answer = low value. Forgotten, wrong within ~6 months, or doesn't transfer when context shifts.
- Changed brain = high value. Solves the next problems, including ones with no answer yet.
- Only need the answer: LLM is fine.
- Will need the knowledge or make decisions on the topic later: a quick answer is not the valuable
  part, even if it feels like a time saving.

## Why it doesn't really save time

- **Generation effect**: an answer generated yourself, even badly or slowly, is remembered far
  better than the same answer read.
- **Desirable difficulty**: the research term for this.
- **Levels of thinking**: remembering, understanding (low); analysing, evaluating, creating (4-6,
  where deep knowledge and strong memory form).
- Asking AI for the answer hands over levels 4-6, leaving level 1: reading.
- Studies of people working with AI: weaker memory, shallower processing, less critical thinking
  than doing the task alone. (no source cited)
- 5 minutes instead of 5 hours, but the ability to think at that level with that knowledge is lost
  for weeks, months or a career.

## When to use AI

- Not anti-AI. Use it where getting good doesn't matter:
  - rough overview of an unfamiliar area before starting
  - dense material into plain language, as a foothold
  - checking reasoning after doing it yourself
- Rule: "Do I want to be better at this in a year?"
  - No: offload without guilt.
  - Yes: think first, AI afterwards.

## Tips

1. **AI after struggling, not before.** Feeling stuck = brain starting useful work. Most people
   resolve the discomfort before any work happens. Write an answer, even a bad one, before asking.
2. **Feeling clear ≠ being clear.** A good explanation feels good because it is well organised, not
   because the knowledge is yours. Nod along, then can't explain it 2 days later.
3. **AI interrogates, doesn't answer.** Have it challenge reasoning, find gaps, or set a harder
   version of the problem. Thinking stays yours and gets harder.
4. **Notice the reach.** For 1 week, log the feeling each time an AI tool is opened: stuck, bored,
   rushed. The reach often comes just before the brain would have done something valuable.

## Self-check

- If every AI output from the last month were deleted, how much would still be in your head? Only
  that was learned.
